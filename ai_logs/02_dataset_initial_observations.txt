Understanding your dataset in easy language
Your dataset is called the Ridgeline Schools Weather Network: 6-week data pack.

Think of it as a collection of records from eight weather stations. Each station measures the weather, records the readings on its computer (called a logger), and stores information about its GPS receiver.

The main purpose of studying this dataset is to find out which readings can be trusted and which may be affected by faulty equipment, incorrect timestamps or data-collection problems.
1. What files did Aeron give you?
Your ZIP file contains 21 files organized into three main groups.

data/ — Weather readings

Contains 8 CSV files, one for each station: S01.csv to S08.csv.

Each file contains temperature, humidity, pressure, wind, rainfall and sunlight measurements.




gps/ — GPS information

Contains 8 NMEA files, one for each station. These hold raw GPS messages that can help us check the station's location and whether its clock is correct.




stations.csv and maintenance_log.md

stations.csv tells us where the stations are located, their elevation and when they were installed.

maintenance_log.md contains volunteers' notes about equipment problems and repairs.



The ZIP also includes a README.md explaining the files and their measurement units.

How frequently is the data collected?
Every 10 minutes.

For example, a station might record readings at 10:00, 10:10, 10:20 and 10:30 UTC.

The dataset covers 42 days, from 1 March to 11 April 2025. Each complete station file contains 6,048 rows, which is 42 days × 24 hours × 6 readings per hour.

4. Why are the GPS files important?
The GPS files contain raw messages from a GPS receiver.

A GPS receiver can help establish:
- Location: Is the station reporting coordinates consistent with its registered location?
- Time: Does the logger's clock agree with the GPS time?
- Signal quality: Are the GPS messages valid, or are they missing or unreliable?
For example, if a station's weather records say 12:00 but its GPS information suggests the clock is several minutes off, you need to investigate before comparing its readings with other stations.

The GPS files are therefore useful evidence, not just extra data to ignore.
